100: 6 Human Evaluation
101: To verify that our automatic evaluation correlates
102: with human judgement, we conduct human eval-
103: uation on selected models and request workers to
104: judge model generations on three dimensions simi-
105: lar to Liu et al. (2023)—(1) utility: a 1-to-5 score
106: indicating whether the generation helps answer the
107: question; (2) citation recall: the annotator is given
108: a sentence and all passages that the sentence cited,
109: and is asked to judge whether the passages fully
110: support the sentence; (3) citation precision: given
111: a sentence and one of its citations, the annotator
112: is asked to judge whether the citation “fully sup-
113: ports”, “partially supports”, or “does not support”
114: the sentence. Each citation gets a precision score
115: 1 if the output sentence has a citation recall of 1
116: and this citation at least “partially supports” it. See
117: Appendix F for more details.

34: 7 Related Work
35: Evaluating citations. Generating text with ci-
36: tations is closely related to attribution. Rashkin
37: et al. (2023) define the “attributable to identified
38: sources” (AIS) score to measure how faithful a gen-
39: erated text is to its sources. Bohnet et al. (2022) ap-
40: ply AIS scores on a single-document short-answer
41: QA dataset. Honovich et al. (2022); Yue et al.
42: (2023) study automatic evaluations for the AIS
43: score. A concurrent work (Liu et al., 2023) conduct
44: human evaluation on commercial generative search
45: engines to examine their citation qualities.
46: Scientific citation text generation (Funkquist
47: et al., 2022) is a related task to ALCE where the
48: model is provided the papers-to-cite and context
49: and is required to recover the citing text. It is differ-
50: ent from ALCE as all citations are provided and the
51: model only needs to perform the summarization.
52: Retrieval-augmented LMs. Many studies have ex-
53: plored augmenting LMs with externally retrieved
54: information. Guu et al. (2020); Borgeaud et al.
55: (2022); Izacard et al. (2022) pre-train language
56: models with retrieved passages, while Khandel-
57: wal et al. (2020); Zhong et al. (2022) augment
58: LLMs’ output by interpolating it with akNN mod-
59: ule; though none of them explicitly provide cita-
60: tions to the retrieved sources. Other works prompt
61: or fine-tune LLMs to “retrieve on-the-fly” (Parisi
62: et al., 2022; Schick et al., 2023; Shuster et al., 2022;
63: Jiang et al., 2023; Yao et al., 2023; Press et al.,
64: 2022), which offers flexibility of when and what to
65: search. Gao et al. (2023); He et al. (2022) propose
66: to first generate text without accessing external doc-
67: uments and then retrieve relevant documents and
68: revise the generation to be consistent.
69: Among previous explorations, Nakano et al.
70: (2021); Menick et al. (2022) are the closest to our
71: setting, where LLMs are trained to answer ques-
72: tions while providing citations. However, they do
73: not explore retrieval strategies and simply use com-
74: mercial search engines, which are not reproducible,
75: and their models and training data are closed-
76: source. To the best of our knowledge, we are the
77: first to implement end-to-end systems that retrieve,
78: synthesize, and cite documents with LLMs.
79: 8 Conclusion
80: We propose ALCE, the first automatic benchmark
81: for evaluating LLM generations with citations. We
82: deploy automatic metrics to measure fluency, cor-
83: rectness, and citation quality, and verify their effi-
84: cacy via human evaluation. We explore a variety of
85: strategies for incorporating citations in LLMs and
86: demonstrate that current systems have considerable
87: room for improvement on ALCE.
88: Our experiments highlight a number of promis-
89: ing research directions, including (1) enhancing re-
90: trieval and refining retrieval integrations in LLMs,
91: (2) developing long-context LLMs, and (3) advanc-
92: ing LLMs’ ability to synthesize multiple sources.
93: What’s even more intriguing is that these research
94: proposals extend beyond the ALCE setup (for ex-
95: ample, long-context LLMs have numerous exciting
96: applications), and ALCE can serve as a valuable
97: testbed for their development.
