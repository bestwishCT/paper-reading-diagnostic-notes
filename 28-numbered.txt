33: 3.4 ALCE is Robust to Shortcut Cases
34: We showcase how the ALCE evaluation is robust
35: to two possible shortcuts in §D: (1) using the top-1
36: retrieved passage as the response and citing itself,
37: and (2) using the first two sentences of the top-1
38: passage. Both cases have almost-perfect citation
39: scores, but (1) has low fluency due to its unnaturally
40: long length compared to human answers, and (2)
41: has low correctness due to low coverage.
42: 4 Modeling
43: In this section, we discuss three major modeling
44: components for an ALCE system—retrieval, syn-
45: thesis, and post-editing.
46: 4.1 Retrieval
47: We explore simple, off-the-shelf retrievers. We use
48: dense retrievers for Wikipedia, including GTR (Ni
49: et al., 2022) and DPR (Karpukhin et al., 2020);
50: we use BM25 for Sphere. For each question, we
51: retrieve the top-100 passages.
52: 4.2 Synthesis
53: We focus on how to prompt an LLM to interact
54: with the retriever, and synthesize and cite the ev-
55: idence (without fine-tuning internal parameters).
56: One noteworthy challenge is that existing LLMs
57: all have limited context window and thus can only
58: fit a handful of passages.
59: VANILLA . We simply provide the model with the
60: top-k9 passages and instruct the model to cite ac-
61: cordingly (Table 2). We also use in-context learn-
62: ing (Brown et al., 2020) and prepend two demon-
63: strations. The complete instruction is in Table 23.
64: SUMM/SNIPPET . With a 4K context window, we
65: can at most safely fitk = 5 passages. As shown in
66: Figure 4, top-5 retrieved passages can only cover
67: 56.8% percent of the answers in ASQA.
68: To tackle this limitation, we propose to provide
69: summaries or snippets of passages instead of the
70: full text (summaries are abstractive but snippets are
71: spans from passages). We acquire summaries and
72: snippets by prompting ChatGPT with instructions
73: (prompts in Table 25 and 26).10 Then we replace
74: all passages with summaries/snippets. Summaries
75: or snippets significantly reduce the passage length,
76: allowing for more passages to fit in: for ASQA,
77: they reduce passage length by 6×on average.
78: Though SUMM/SNIPPET allows for more re-
79: trieved passages, they are lossy compressions. To
80: alleviate this problem, we propose INTERACT , an
81: interactive prompting scheme to allow the model to
82: check the full text of certain passages. At each step,
83: the model can execute one of three actions: (1)
84: “Check: Document [1][2]” to check the full text
85: of the corresponding documents; (2) “Output:” to
86: output a statement of the answer; (3) “End.” to end
87: the generation. §C provides more details.
88: INLINE SEARCH . The above methods all dis-
89: play retrieval results at the beginning. In INLI -
90: NESEARCH , we allow LLMs to call “search” dur-
91: ing the generation process (Yao et al., 2023; Press
92: et al., 2022; Jiang et al., 2023). At each step, the
93: model can execute one of three actions: “Search:

27: 4.3 Post-editing
28: In this section we discuss two strategies for refining
29: the output to further improve its quality.
30: RERANK . We randomly sample nsample = 4 re-
31: sponses for each question, and select the best re-
32: sponse using the automatic citation recall score.
33: we expect RERANK to improve the citation quality.
34: POST CITE. For each statement, we find the best
35: matching passage among the top-100 retrieved pas-
36: sages using GTR and cite it. We combine this with
37: CLOSED BOOK in our experiments.
